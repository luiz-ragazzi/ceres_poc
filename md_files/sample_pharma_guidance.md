# Guidance for Industry: Process Validation for Sterile Drug Products (Sample)

Document ID: SAMPLE-GUID-0001
Version: Draft 1.0
Effective Date: 2026-01-15

## 1. Purpose and Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

This guidance describes a synthetic framework for validating manufacturing processes for sterile drug products, for use in testing document retrieval systems.

### 1.2 Scope

This guidance applies to sample manufacturing sites producing sterile injectable products by aseptic processing or terminal sterilization. It does not apply to non-sterile solid oral dosage forms, which are addressed in a separate sample guidance (SAMPLE-GUID-0002).

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Process Validation

Process validation means the collection and evaluation of data, from the process design stage through commercial production, which establishes scientific evidence that a process is capable of consistently delivering quality product.

### 2.2 Critical Process Parameter (CPP)

A process parameter whose variability has an impact on a critical quality attribute and therefore should be monitored or controlled to ensure the process produces the desired quality.

### 2.3 Media Fill

A simulation of the aseptic manufacturing process using a sterile microbiological growth medium in place of the drug product, used to assess the risk of microbial contamination.

## 3. General Principles

### 3.1 Lifecycle Approach

Process validation should be considered across three stages:

- Stage 1 — Process Design
- Stage 2 — Process Qualification
- Stage 3 — Continued Process Verification

### 3.2 Risk-Based Approach

Manufacturers should apply quality risk management principles, as described in Section 5.3, when determining the extent and rigor of validation activities.

## 4. Stage 1: Process Design

### 4.1 Establishing a Strategy

A process design strategy should be based on knowledge gained through development and scale-up activities. Sources of process knowledge include prior platform experience, risk analysis, and small-scale studies.

### 4.2 Identification of Critical Process Parameters

#### 4.2.1 Risk Assessment

Manufacturers should conduct a documented risk assessment to identify which process parameters are critical. This assessment should consider the parameter's relationship to a critical quality attribute (CQA), as defined in Section 2.2, and the process step in which it occurs.

#### 4.2.2 Design of Experiments

Where feasible, a design of experiments (DOE) approach should be used to characterize the relationship between process parameters and quality attributes across the operating range.

### 4.3 Sample Acceptance Criteria Table

The following table illustrates example (synthetic) acceptance criteria for a sample sterile fill-finish process:

| Parameter | Target | Acceptable Range | Test Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fill Volume | 5.0 mL | 4.9 - 5.1 mL | Gravimetric |
| Bioburden (pre-filtration) | 0 CFU/mL | ≤ 10 CFU/100 mL | USP <61> (sample) |
| Container Closure Integrity | Pass | Pass, 0 leaks | Dye Ingress (sample) |
| Sterility | Sterile | No growth at 14 days | USP <71> (sample) |

## 5. Stage 2: Process Qualification

### 5.1 Facility and Equipment Qualification

Facilities, utilities, and equipment used in the process should be qualified prior to process performance qualification (PPQ), following the sequence: Design Qualification (DQ), Installation Qualification (IQ), Operational Qualification (OQ), and Performance Qualification (PQ).

### 5.2 Process Performance Qualification

#### 5.2.1 Media Fill Studies

Media fill studies, as defined in Section 2.3, should be conducted to simulate worst-case aseptic processing conditions, including maximum permitted interventions and maximum campaign duration.

Media fill studies should generally be conducted in triplicate for a new line, with a minimum of three consecutive successful runs required before the line is considered qualified. This requirement applies only to the initial qualification described above and not to routine semi-annual requalification, which is addressed in Section 6.2.

#### 5.2.2 Number of PPQ Batches

The number of PPQ batches should be justified based on the risk assessment referenced in Section 4.2.1. A minimum of three consecutive batches is generally expected unless a documented rationale supports an alternative approach.

### 5.3 Quality Risk Management

Risk assessment tools such as Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA) should be applied consistently across Stage 1 and Stage 2 activities to focus validation resources on the highest-risk process steps.

## 6. Stage 3: Continued Process Verification

### 6.1 Ongoing Monitoring

An ongoing program should be established to collect and analyze data related to product and process performance, allowing detection of undesired process variability.

### 6.2 Requalification

#### 6.2.1 Routine Requalification

Aseptic processing lines should undergo requalification, including media fill studies, at a minimum semi-annual frequency, or more frequently if triggered by the events described in Section 6.2.2.

#### 6.2.2 Triggered Requalification

Requalification should also be triggered by, at minimum: a change in primary packaging components, a change in fill line configuration, or any sterility test failure.

## 7. Documentation

### 7.1 Validation Master Plan

A Validation Master Plan (VMP) should describe the overall philosophy, intention, and approach to be used for establishing performance adequacy.

### 7.2 Validation Report

Upon completion of validation activities, a summary report should be prepared, referencing the acceptance criteria in Section 4.3 and documenting whether those criteria were met.
